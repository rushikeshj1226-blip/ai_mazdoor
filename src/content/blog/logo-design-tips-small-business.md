Your logo shows up everywhere — on your shop board, visiting card, WhatsApp DP, packaging and Google listing. A good one makes you look established from day one. A weak one quietly makes customers trust you a little less.

Whether you design it yourself or hire a designer, here are six rules professionals follow.

## 1. Simple beats clever

The most recognisable logos are simple enough to sketch from memory. Avoid stuffing every idea — a cup, a leaf, a star and your initials — into one mark. Pick **one strong idea** and execute it well.

> If you can’t describe your logo in one sentence, it is probably too complicated.

## 2. It must work tiny and huge

Your logo will appear as a 40-pixel profile picture and on a 10-foot hoarding. Fine details and thin lines disappear at small sizes. A professional logo system includes a **primary logo, a compact version and an icon**, so it stays clear everywhere.

## 3. Test it in black and white first

Colour is powerful, but a strong logo works in a single colour — think of rubber stamps, newspaper ads or embroidered uniforms. Designers judge the shape first and add colour later.

## 4. Choose colours with meaning

Colours carry feelings:

- **Orange** — energy, warmth, friendliness
- **Blue** — trust and reliability
- **Green** — health, nature, freshness
- **Black** — premium and bold

Pick a main colour and one or two supporting colours, then use them consistently everywhere.

## 5. Think bilingual if your customers are

Many Indian brands speak to customers in both Hindi and English. A well-designed **bilingual logo** — Devanagari and Latin lettering that feel like one family — builds instant connection in local markets while still looking modern. It needs careful typography, because Hindi letters have different heights, curves and the shirorekha (top line).

## 6. Make sure it is original

Downloading a free icon or copying a famous brand’s style can get you into trouble — and it will never feel like *yours*. An original logo can also be **trademarked**, protecting your brand as you grow.

## What you should receive from a logo designer

A professional logo project should give you more than one PNG file:

1. The logo in vector formats (AI, SVG, PDF) and PNG/JPG
2. Colour and black/white versions
3. An icon / favicon version
4. Your brand colours with codes
5. The fonts used
6. Ideally, a short brand guideline document
7. **Full copyright** in your name

## DIY or hire a designer?

Free logo makers are fine for a weekend project. But if you are investing in a shop, a website, packaging or ads, your logo is the foundation of all of it. A professionally designed identity usually pays for itself through the trust it builds.
