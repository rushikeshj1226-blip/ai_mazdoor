“Website banwani hai” is where most projects start — and where many go wrong. Without a clear plan, you end up with a site that looks fine but brings no enquiries. Decide these seven things first, and any good designer can build you a website that actually works for your business.

## 1. What should the website *do*?

A website is not a digital visiting card. Pick one main goal:

- Get **WhatsApp enquiries or calls**
- Get **appointments or bookings**
- **Sell products** online
- Build **trust** before a sales meeting

Every page, button and line of text should push visitors towards that one goal.

## 2. Which pages do you really need?

Most small businesses need fewer pages than they think. A strong starting point:

1. **Home** — who you are, what you do, why trust you
2. **Services / products** — one clear page per main service
3. **About** — your story and your team
4. **Contact** — WhatsApp, phone, map and a short form

Add a **blog** if you want to grow on Google over time.

## 3. Who writes the content?

Content is the most common reason websites get delayed. Decide early whether you will write it, or whether your designer should help. Simple, honest language — in English, Hindi or both — works better than corporate jargon.

> Customers don’t read websites. They scan them. Short sentences win.

## 4. Template or custom design?

A template gets you online quickly, but your website will look like thousands of others. A **custom design** is planned around your customers and your brand, which usually builds more trust and converts better. If your logo and brand colours are not ready yet, sort those out first.

## 5. Will it be fast on a phone?

Most of your visitors will open your site on a mobile phone, often on a patchy connection. Ask your designer:

- Is the design **mobile-first**?
- How will images and fonts be **optimised**?
- What **Google PageSpeed** score do you aim for?

A slow website quietly loses customers before they see a single word.

## 6. Is it ready for Google?

SEO is not something you “add later”. A good build includes:

- A clear heading structure and unique **meta title and description** for every page
- **Schema markup** so Google understands your business
- A **sitemap**, Google Search Console and Google Business Profile setup

## 7. Who owns what?

This is the question most people forget to ask:

- Is the **domain registered in your name**?
- Do you get the **website files and logins**?
- Can you **edit text and photos yourself** without calling a developer?

If the answer to any of these is “no”, think twice.

## Red flags when hiring a web designer

- **No written scope.** If pages, features and revisions are not written down, expect surprises later.
- **No questions about your business.** A good designer asks about your customers before talking about colours.
- **No mention of mobile speed or SEO.**
- **No support after launch.**
